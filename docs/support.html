Migraine Log

Support

Migraine Log (on the App Store as Migraine Tracker for Headaches) is a headache and migraine diary for iPhone: one tap when an attack starts, pain, symptoms and medications when you feel better, and a report for your doctor.

How do I log an attack?

Tap Attack started on the Log screen. The time is saved. Mild, Moderate and Severe under the button are optional. When it is over, tap Attack over. Started one by mistake? Tap Started by mistake under the clock. Closed it too early? Tap Not over yet right after.

Can I start one from the home screen?

Yes. Touch and hold the Migraine Log icon and choose Attack started (or Attack over while one is open). You can also make a Shortcut with the action Open URL and the address migrainelog://start, and run it with Siri.

How do I add details or a past attack?

Tap an attack on the Log screen, in All attacks or on a calendar day. Everything is optional and saved as you go. To enter one from memory, tap Add a past attack, pick the day and time and how long it lasted.

Why mark headache-free days?

The counts are only honest when the good days are logged too. Tap a day in the calendar and switch on No headache this day, or answer No to "Headache today?" in the evening. Days without any entry are never counted as headache-free.

How are migraine days counted?

A day with a moderate or severe attack, or one you marked as a migraine. You can say an attack was not a migraine in its details. A day with an attack of any kind is a headache day.

How do I make the report?

Open Report, choose 30, 90 or 180 days, add a name if you like, and tap Preview the report. With Pro, tap Share PDF or Print. Free users can see the preview, marked SAMPLE.

Can I get my data out?

With Pro, Report or Settings, All attacks as a spreadsheet (CSV), gives one row per attack.

How do I restore a purchase?

Settings, Restore purchases. Make sure you are signed in with the Apple Account you bought Migraine Log Pro with.

Is this medical advice?

No. Migraine Log is a personal log. It does not diagnose, treat, or give medical or dosing advice, and it does not predict attacks. Talk to your doctor about your headaches and your medications.
